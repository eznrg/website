Hospitality energy strategy

A hotel is a distinct energy system.

A hotel is not an office building with beds. It is a 24/7 service factory that turns energy into hot showers, clean linens, comfortable rooms, and time together. A durable strategy is not simply about using less. It is about using the right energy, at the right time, in ways guests never notice.

Why hotels are different

Energy is part of the guest experience.

U.S. lodging represents just 3% of commercial buildings but 9% of fuels consumption. Water heating and space heating alone account for nearly 40% of hotel energy use. Sources: U.S. DOE Better Buildings and EIA 2018 CBECS. See citations 1–2.

Energy is a product input

Conditioned rooms, hot water, laundry, food service, and amenities all turn energy into the experience a guest buys.

Occupancy is not the whole story

Weather, service intensity, equipment schedules, and operating choices can make two equally occupied nights look entirely different.

The monthly bill hides the answer

A bill shows how much was used—not when cost was created, where flexibility exists, or which system drove the change.

Regional evidence · ComEd territory

The load shape tells the story.

Across four metered lodging properties, every annual peak arrived in January—during a morning or evening ramp hour, not a summer afternoon.

That pattern is not a national benchmark. It is a clear example of why a hotel should be understood on its own terms before anyone prescribes equipment or promises savings.

A disciplined sequence

Five steps, in this order.

Capital comes last—after the property has established how its load behaves and where flexibility has real economic value.

  1. 01MeasureCombine interval, weather, and occupancy data.
  2. 02ModelForecast load and identify anomalies.
  3. 03ControlAct within guest-comfort guardrails.
  4. 04MonetizeCapture procurement, peak, and grid value.
  5. 05BuildSize physical assets only after the value is clear.
